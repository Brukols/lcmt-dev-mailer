ALTCHA, the built-in spam protection, moves to ALTCHA 3.5 and gets a **Difficulty** setting. It is still invisible to visitors, needs no account and sends nothing to a third party.

## What changes

- **Stronger ALTCHA protection.** The challenge is now a PBKDF2 calculation, issued and checked by your own site. Each proof still works once and expires after 5 minutes.
- **New Difficulty setting**, in **Email templates → Spam protection → ALTCHA**: *Light*, *Normal* or *High*. It sets how much work the visitor's browser does before a form can be sent: about 0.3 s, 1 s and 3 s on a recent computer. **Normal** is recommended and is the default. Older phones can take a few seconds at *High*, so only raise it if you see a lot of spam.
- **Nothing to set up**: your ALTCHA key is kept, and so are your choices of spam protection per form.
- **Privacy is unchanged**: the calculation runs in the visitor's browser, no data goes to a third-party service, no cookie is set, and the widget records no mouse or keyboard activity. The text added to your privacy policy is still accurate.
- **Heavier script**: the ALTCHA script grows from about 71 KB to about 125 KB, and only loads on pages with a form it protects.

## Updating

From the WordPress admin: Plugins → update "LCMT Mailer". Nothing is required from you.

- **A form opened just before the update must be sent again**: a visitor who already had it open gets the spam protection error once, and can send it a second time after reloading the page. Updating at a quiet time avoids it.
- **Purge your page cache** (and your optimisation plugin's cache) after updating, so cached pages carry the new markup. Pages cached before the update keep working in the meantime.
- If your site sends a Content-Security-Policy header that restricts `worker-src`, allow `blob:` in it: ALTCHA's calculation runs in workers created from `blob:` URLs.

## For developers

- New option `lcmt_mailer_altcha_difficulty` (`low`, `normal`, `high`), removed with the plugin's data on uninstall.
- The challenge and proof format changed (ALTCHA's "v2" protocol: `{ challenge: { parameters, signature }, solution }`), see `.claude/architecture.md`. Anything that posts a hand-made `altcha` value must use it.
